# 3. Sub-Domain and Problem Identification

## 3.1 Sub-Domain Selected

Within the domain of hostel management this project addresses the sub-domain of mess management specifically the publishing of the menu, the collection of student feedback on meals and the administration of mess fee rebates at Hostel D (Neeram Hall), Thapar Institute of Engineering and Technology, Patiala, which serves close to 800 students across three meals a day.

## 3.2 The Existing Process

Three separate arrangements are currently in use. The menu is announced on hostel WhatsApp groups. Rebates are claimed on a printed form handed in at the warden's office. Feedback and complaints are submitted through a web form opened by a QR code displayed in the mess. Each was introduced independently to solve one problem and none of them was designed to work with the others.

## 3.3 Problems Identified

### Problem 1 — The menu is announced but not recorded

A WhatsApp message is read once and then buried under later messages. A student who wants to check the menu for dinner at five o'clock has to scroll back through the group to find it. Worse, when a menu is changed the revised message looks exactly like the original, so there is no way to tell what was finally served. Since no dated record survives a complaint about a meal cannot afterwards be matched to the dish it concerned.

### Problem 2 — Rebate applications cannot be tracked

Once the printed rebate form is submitted, the student has no further contact with the process. There is no acknowledgement that the form was received, no indication of whether it was approved or rejected and no statement of how many rebate days were eventually credited. If the form is misplaced neither the student nor the office can establish that it was ever submitted and the loss usually surfaces only when the mess bill arrives.

### Problem 3 — Feedback is collected but never closed

The QR-linked form allows students to report a complaint or rate a meal but nothing returns to them. They receive no confirmation. They never learn what action was taken and the responses are not summarised or published. The committee receives a list of individual comments with no means of aggregating them, so a dish disliked by many students appears no different from one disliked by a single student. Students who see no result from complaining eventually stop complaining and the little feedback that continues to arrive becomes an unreliable measure of what is actually wrong.

## 3.4 The Underlying Issue

These three problems share a common cause. The menu, the rebates and the feedback are held in three separate places that cannot read one another. A rating cannot be attached to the meal it describes, feedback cannot be compared across weeks and a rebate cannot be checked against any record of attendance. The data needed to manage the mess sensibly already exists. It is simply scattered.

## 3.5 The Proposed Solution

The proposed system brings these functions together in a single application with role-based access for four users — student, mess committee, warden and mess contractor. Students view a permanently recorded daily menu, rate meals, raise complaints and apply for rebates online. The committee updates the menu and acts on aggregated feedback. The warden approves or rejects rebate applications with each decision recorded and communicated automatically. The contractor receives consolidated reports on ratings and complaints. Because every function draws on the same records, feedback can be linked to the meal itconcerns, rebate applications carry a visible status from submission todecision and decisions about the mess can rest on recorded data rather than recollection.
